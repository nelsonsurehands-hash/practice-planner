1-3-1 Zone Offense vs. 2-3

A 2-3 zone has two defenders up top and three across the baseline, which leaves gaps at the free-throw line and on the wings. The 1-3-1 alignment puts a player in each gap: a guard at the top between the two guards, wings on each side, a big at the free-throw line, and a baseline runner. Get the ball to the high post and the zone has to collapse, which opens the wings and the baseline.

Type
Zone offense
Level
Middle school to varsity

How to run it

  1. 1-3-1 against the 2-3: 1 splits the two top defenders, 4 sits at the free-throw line in the middle of the zone. 1 passes to 2 on the wing.
  2. 5 runs the baseline to the ball-side short corner, and 2 hits 4 at the high post, the soft spot of the 2-3.
  3. 4 turns and reads the zone: 5 under the basket, or 3 on the weak-side wing. The back line shifted to the ball, so 4 finds 3 for the open shot.

When to use it

Whenever a team sits in a 2-3 zone. The same alignment works against a 2-1-2 and gives your players a simple rule: get the ball inside the zone, then find the open shooter.

Coaching points

  • Sit in the gaps between defenders, never right in front of one.
  • The high post catches facing the basket, or turns immediately. That turn is what collapses the zone.
  • Use ball fakes: a fake one way moves the zone, then pass the other way.
  • Skip passes beat zones. Look over the top to the weak-side wing.
  • Crash the boards from the weak side; zones have trouble boxing out.

Reads

  • High post catches and the middle defender steps up: pass to 5 on the baseline.
  • Back line shifts to the ball: skip it to the weak-side wing.
  • Top defenders sink to the high post: 1 is open for the shot at the top.
  • Wing defender closes out hard: one dribble into the gap and pull up.
